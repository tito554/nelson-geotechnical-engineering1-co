Service characteristics in Nelson

A retaining wall project along Rocks Road forced the team to rethink the anchor layout entirely. The cut faced the bay, with a tidally influenced groundwater table and schist dipping unfavourably toward the excavation. The initial design called for a grid of active multistrand anchors at 3-metre centres. During installation the first three anchors lost grout into open joints—a clear sign the rock mass was more fractured than the borehole logs suggested. The solution paired passive rock dowels in the upper weathered zone with deeper active anchors socketed into competent greywacke below 12 metres. Each active anchor was stressed in three load increments, holding at each step to measure displacement with a dial gauge reading to 0.01 mm. The proof test protocol followed NZS 3404, requiring less than 2 mm of creep over a 10-minute hold at 1.5 times design load. Six anchors failed the first cycle and needed re-grouting. The second cycle passed. That wall has now stood through two major storm surges and a 5.8 magnitude shake without a millimetre of lateral movement.

Critical ground factors in Nelson

The testing rig itself tells the story. A hollow-stem auger spins into the cut, a strand is fed down to the designed bond zone, and high-pressure grout fills the annular space. In Nelson's hillside subdivisions, that rig is often working on a battered bench with barely enough setback for the power pack. The biggest technical headache is confirming grout-to-ground bond in variable rock. A 15-metre anchor in the Moutere Gravel might proof-test beautifully at 1.5 times working load. Shift twenty metres into the weathered schist and the same design can show creep beyond the 2 mm criterion in the first cycle. That's when passive anchors become a safer bet—they rely on progressive load transfer along the full bonded length, forgiving localised weak zones. Corrosion protection is another layer. The marine aerosol from Tasman Bay drifts inland over The Wood and Nelson South, accelerating attack on exposed anchor heads. Double-corrosion-protected (DCP) systems are non-negotiable here, with encapsulation from the internal wedge to the trumpet.

Our services

Anchor design in the Nelson-Tasman region typically branches into two distinct service tracks, shaped by the site geology and the performance required from the retained structure.

Active anchor design and proof testing

Full design of prestressed strand anchors with lock-off procedures, load cell monitoring, and staged proof testing according to NZS 3404. Suited for retaining walls, bridge abutments, and any structure where immediate load transfer and minimal deformation are critical.

Passive rock dowel and soil nail systems

Design of fully grouted passive reinforcement for cut slopes, rock faces, and excavations in weathered schist or colluvium. Includes pull-out testing on sacrificial nails to confirm bond strength before production installation begins.

Seismic site assessment in Nelson addresses the critical need to understand how local ground conditions influence earthquake shaking and foundation performance. The region’s geology is dominated by Moutere Gravels, alluvial deposits of the Waimea Plains, and estuarine silts near Nelson Haven, all of which respond very differently during a seismic event. Site-specific studies must comply with the requirements of the New Zealand Building Code, particularly Clause B1 (Structure), and follow the guidance of MBIE/NZGS Module 1 for seismic parameters and Module 2 for geotechnical investigation, ensuring foundation designs align with the NZS 1170.5 seismic actions standard. Our geotechnical investigation services provide the essential data to classify site subsoil class in accordance with NZS 1170.5, while targeted CPT testing rapidly delineates soil behaviour type and cyclic resistance in these variable sediments.

Our methodology relies on the rigorous framework established by New Zealand standards and accepted industry practice. Field campaigns begin with a combination of cone penetration tests and borehole drilling to capture continuous stratigraphic profiles, with shear wave velocity (Vs) measured directly via seismic CPT or downhole methods to satisfy Section 3 of NZS 1170.5. In boreholes, we perform In-Situ such as Standard Penetration Tests (SPT) and collect undisturbed samples for advanced laboratory evaluation. The laboratory programme, conducted to NZGS guidelines, typically includes cyclic triaxial testing, resonant column tests, and index property determination such as Atterberg limits and grain size analysis to assess liquefaction susceptibility. This integrated field and laboratory approach provides the parameters required for rigorous seismic response analysis, from simplified liquefaction triggering procedures through to two-dimensional dynamic finite element modelling.

Typical projects in Nelson where seismic assessment is fundamental range from residential subdivisions on the sloping Moutere Gravel terrain to commercial developments on the deep alluvial soils of Stoke and Richmond, and port-related infrastructure near the waterfront. The high groundwater table across much of the Waimea Plains elevates liquefaction risk, making detailed assessment mandatory for medium- and high-importance structures. For lightly loaded residential foundations, seismic demand often governs the bearing capacity and settlement checks, while larger industrial projects require time-history analysis to design deep foundations that mitigate kinematic and inertial effects. Our team has extensive experience with the specific depositional environments of the Tasman region, allowing us to recognise problematic units such as loose, saturated sands within the Appleby Gravels that are prone to cyclic softening.

The seismic assessment process delivers a clear, actionable site subsoil classification and a suite of geotechnical parameters for structural design. Clients receive a comprehensive report containing seismic hazard context, field and laboratory data, liquefaction assessment results, and modulus degradation curves where advanced testing is performed. We provide explicit recommendations for Improvement if required and practical foundation options calibrated to the performance expectations of NZS 1170.5. By integrating local geological knowledge with the full spectrum of investigation techniques—from field density testing for compaction control to sophisticated laboratory dynamic testing—we deliver certainty on a site’s seismic performance, enabling informed decision-making and code-compliant design in Nelson’s demanding seismic environment.
